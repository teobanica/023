# Crossing distances and quantum reflection groups

**Codex, Banica and Claude**, November 2026

The easy quantum groups $U_N\subset U_N^D\subset U_N^+$ of Mang and Weber come from pairings whose strings may cross only at certain distances, and are indexed by the additive semigroups $D$ of natural numbers. To each of them we associate a quantum reflection group $H_N\subset H_N^{D'}\subset H_N^+$, generated by $H_N$ and by diagonal group duals, coming from reflections placed on the integer line, which commute exactly at the distances in $D$. On the diagonal torus of $U_N^D$ the rule is the opposite one, with commutation exactly at the distances outside $D$.

- The correspondence $U_N^D\to H_N^{D'}$ is one-to-one and reverses inclusions, and its image is described explicitly. The semigroup $D$ can be read on 3 diagonal coordinates of $U_N^D$. With $m$ being the smallest positive element of $D$, and with $t=1$ or $t=2$ depending on whether $0$ belongs to $D$ or not, the reflection partner has free diagonal entries exactly in the dimensions $N\leq tm$.
- When $D$ contains $0$, the 2 partners generate $U_N^+$, in any dimension $N\geq3$.
- The limiting main characters are a sum of a Poisson number of reflections, and a shifted mixed Gaussian variable. Certain moments of the unitary character count the order-preserving maps into noncrossing partitions, and the sets of pairwise free reflections. Apart from 2 exceptions, all the reflection partners have the same moments up to order 29. For the path, where only the distance 1 is outside $D$, the moment of order 30 exceeds the free one by 10.
- For numerical semigroups, the first dimension where 2 reflection partners differ is computed in several cases, and is at most $2g^3$ in general, with $g$ being the largest gap. For gaps up to 12 it is at most $g+1$, by a computer check, and this is conjectured to hold in general.

## Running the code

The Python programs need Python 3.10 or later, with no extra packages. The other 2 programs need a C++ compiler supporting C++20. From the repository root, reproduce the census of relations for the path with

```sh
c++ -O2 -std=c++20 path-moments.cpp -o path-moments && ./path-moments
```

The program finds 10 patterns on 3 letters at length 30, no candidate passing the tests on 4 to 7 letters, and prints the moments up to order 30. The last line gives 2376234441556411 for the free case, and 2376234441556421 for the path.

For the check on the pairs of numerical semigroups, run

```sh
c++ -O2 -std=c++17 dimension.cpp -o dimension && ./dimension 12
```

This takes about one minute up to gaps 11, and several more minutes for gaps 12. There are 14535 pairs in total, and each line of the output ends with `placements found 0`.

The test of the edge bound conjecture, on all the subsets of $\lbrace 0,\ldots,18\rbrace$ containing $0$, for the semigroups with gaps up to 9, is

```sh
python3 isoperimetry.py 9 18
```

It tests 14942208 sets, with 0 failures, in about 20 seconds. Finally, the 2 formulas for the character laws are checked with

```sh
python3 orders.py
python3 cumulants.py 8
```

The first program prints the 2 counts side by side, for 7 semigroups. The second one prints the moments and the free cumulants up to order 8, for 8 semigroups, and takes about 3 minutes at order 10.
